## Bacterial Leaf Blight (BLB)

**Symptoms**
- Leaf edges look yellow or dried.
- Leaf area can look scorched from the tip or margin.

**Drivers**
- Irrigation water flowing between plots can speed bacterial spread.
- Rain or strong wind helps symptoms move.

**Safe steps**
- Take 2–3 extra photos from different leaves and hills.
- Note location, crop age, and how widely symptoms appear.
- Keep separate notes for plots that show similar symptoms.
- Reduce movement of diseased plant debris between plots.
- Watch the field after rain or strong wind.
- Use symptom notes when talking with an extension officer; ask for
  verification before further control.

## Blast

**Symptoms**
- Grey or brown spots on leaves, often with a darker edge.
- Spots can be diamond-shaped and appear in clusters on several leaves.

**Drivers**
- High humidity and a prolonged wet canopy favor blast.
- Dense planting raises risk.

**Safe steps**
- Take close-up photos and a hill-distance photo.
- Note whether symptoms appear at several points in the field.
- Watch humidity and plant density; keep diseased residue clean.
- Repeat observations to see whether spread is increasing.
- Ask for verification if spots spread quickly; ask an extension officer
  before chemical control or a large culture change.

## Brown Spot

**Symptoms**
- Small brown spots on leaves; some spots have a lighter centre.
- Spread can be uneven from leaf to leaf.

**Drivers**
- Moisture stress or water shortage can trigger brown spot.
- Nutrient or environmental stress can look similar.

**Safe steps**
- Take extra photos of older and younger leaves.
- Note field, water, and affected area; compare with nearby plants.
- Watch plants that show new spots; handle diseased residue hygienically.
- Discuss possible nutrient factors with an extension officer; confirm
  before choosing a control.

## Tungro

**Symptoms**
- Leaves look yellow or orange.
- Plants can look stunted; symptoms often start on certain hills then spread.

**Drivers**
- Spread by brown planthopper as the tungro vector.
- Similar symptoms in neighbouring fields mark spread risk.

**Safe steps**
- Photograph the whole hill and the yellowing leaves.
- Note whether the pattern appears on adjacent plots and when it started.
- Report to an extension officer or field staff if cases repeat; coordinate
  because spread risk needs a wider look.

Aliases: hawar daun, bercak cokelat, penyakit, daun padi, triase, penyuluh, wereng.
